\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Rapid infrastructure expansion, urban gigaprojects, and agricultural initiatives across the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia under Vision 2030 require automated, scalable, and continuous environmental monitoring. However, satellite-based change detection in hyper-arid environments faces severe scientific challenges: high seasonal sun angle variations, soil moisture shifts, sabkha salinity, and spectral similarities between urban concrete and natural limestone deserts frequently trigger catastrophic false alert rates in conventional algorithms, untuned computer vision models, and even standalone global satellite foundation models.

This capstone project presents \textbf{TerraShift AI}, a production-ready, full-stack geospatial platform designed to identify, locate, measure, and analyze authentic land-surface changes by fusing bitemporal Siamese deep learning, Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A multispectral surface reflectance, and authentic Google AlphaEarth V1 64-dimensional satellite foundation-model embeddings (\texttt{GOOGLE/SATELLITE\_EMBEDDING/V1/ANNUAL}) alongside Google Dynamic World V1 (\texttt{GOOGLE/DYNAMICWORLD/V1}). To resolve the central research question—\textit{whether physics-guided hybrid foundation architectures genuinely outperform standalone global foundation models and conventional remote sensing across diverse hyper-arid environments}—we conduct a controlled, head-to-head empirical evaluation across four Saudi reference corridors totaling $91.19\text{ km}^2$: (1) Riyadh ROSHN Sedra (residential earthworks on limestone desert), (2) Jeddah North Obhur (coastal infill and sabkha), (3) Al-Jouf Wadi As-Sirhan (center-pivot agricultural irrigation), and (4) an entirely held-out test corridor in Al-Kharj (logistics warehouse development and oasis activation).

Methodologically, we prohibit synthetic benchmark fallbacks (\texttt{allow\_synthetic=False}), enforce exact geometric AOI clipping in WGS84 prior to ellipsoidal geodesic area integration, and formulate a **Localization-Aware Greedy $1$-to-$1$ Bipartite Event-Matching Protocol**. Rather than relying on naive intersection or arbitrary area caps, a predicted polygon must satisfy both physical coverage ($\text{Area}(P \cap G) \ge 3,500\text{ m}^2$ or $\text{RoC} \ge 0.08$) and localization purity ($\text{PoC} \ge 0.15$ and $\text{IoU} \ge 0.05$, or $\text{PoC} \ge 0.30$ for compact surgical detections), audited across a four-regime sensitivity sweep (\textit{Relaxed}, \textit{Standard}, \textit{Moderate}, \textit{Strict}) and reported with separate **Pooled (micro)** and **Corridor Macro-Averaged** metrics.

Empirical results on authenticated Google Earth Engine and AWS Earth Search STAC imagery establish a clear hierarchy across the seven evaluated paradigms:
(1) \textbf{TerraShift v6.0 Hybrid (Siamese + AlphaEarth 64-D + L2A Physical Guards)} achieves Rank \#1 with **79.2\% Pooled Event F1** (**82.5\% Macro F1**), **65.5\% Pooled Strict Precision** (**70.1\% Macro Precision**; reaching 100.0\% in Jeddah and 71.4\% in Riyadh), and **100.0\% Recall** (19/19 events, including 100\% recall and 62.5\% precision on Held-Out Al-Kharj), while cutting false alarms by **91.2\%** (from 114 down to 10) and maintaining **0 Negative Control Zone hits**.
(2) \textbf{TerraShift v5.1 (Tuned Siamese + L2A Spectral Guards, Zero GEE Dependency)} achieves Rank \#2 with **53.5\% Pooled Event F1** (**64.1\% Macro F1**), **36.5\% Pooled Precision** (**47.2\% Macro Precision**), **100.0\% Recall**, a **71.1\% false-alarm reduction** (114 to 33), and **0 Negative Control Zone hits**, providing 5-day tactical monitoring between annual foundation-model releases.
(3) In contrast, standalone \textbf{Google AlphaEarth V1 Cosine Dissimilarity ($\tau=0.12$)} achieves only **27.0\% Pooled F1** (**31.4\% Macro F1**) with **90 false alarms** and **23 false alarms inside undisturbed desert Negative Control Zones**, while standalone \textbf{Google Dynamic World V1 LULC} achieves only **19.7\% Pooled F1** (**31.6\% Recall**) because global LULC classifiers confuse bright desert regolith with concrete grading.

Architecturally, TerraShift AI combines Next.js 16 (React 19, MapLibre GL, TypeScript strict mode) with a Python FastAPI inference engine, executing locally on commodity CPU hardware in **1.485 seconds** of pure compute time per corridor with a 301 MB RAM footprint and zero cloud GPU cost.
